# GI Solid Rolling Shutter — master visual definition

Governs the generated gallery views for the GI Solid Rolling Shutter product
page. The approved hero,
`public/images/products/rolling-shutters/galvanized-steel-rolling-shutters/galvanized-steel-rolling-shutters-supplied.webp`,
is the reference and is not altered.

Generated 2026-09-19 through Google Flow (Nano Banana Pro, 2K download).
The hero fixed the curtain; the supplied MS shutter reference fixed the
drive. Dinesh asked that the gallery differentiate GI from MS through
engineering close-ups rather than full-shutter photographs, and pointed to
Gandhi Automations' galvanised shutter page as the installation idiom:
large industrial bays in pre-engineered steel sheds. Candidates were
previewed; four approved and installed (slat profile stack and material
macro generated but not selected).

## The product, as fixed by the hero and the product copy

- **Curtain** — curved convex roll-formed galvanised steel interlocking
  slats, 75–80 mm class, in the natural hot-dip galvanised finish: a matte
  grey zinc surface with a faint spangle, never chrome or painted.
- **Guides** — heavy galvanised angle guides bolted to the structure
  (reinforced on the heavy-duty configuration); galvanised angle bottom rail.
- **Head** — curtain wound on a steel barrel carried in bearings on
  galvanised end plates on column brackets (the hero shows a galvanised
  hood over the same arrangement).
- **Drive** — geared side motor on the end plate with hand chain and red
  and green pull cords.

## Setting

Pre-engineered steel factory shed: grey metal cladding, exposed steel
columns and purlins, concrete floor with yellow markings, yellow-and-black
bollards, neutral daylight.

## Rules

Same slat profile, galvanised finish, guides, barrel arrangement and drive
in every view. A painted MS, aluminium, perforated or grille curtain is
rejected. No text, dimensions, stickers, logos, brand names, model numbers
or certification marks; no people or vehicles; no specification implied
visually. Interim visualisations, replaced by installation photography under
the same filenames when it exists.
